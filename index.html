About Me

Hello — I'm Yogesh Bhardwaj. By day, across three remote roles, I evaluate and red-team large language models for a living. In parallel, as a PhD researcher, I build the mathematics behind published cosmology. Most people bring one half of that. I bring both, and I've stopped being surprised at how often they overlap.


I'm an Applied Mathematics PhD researcher at Delhi Technological University, working on how the universe expands — matter creation cosmology, modified gravity, dark energy — some of the largest structures in existence, explained through some of the most demanding mathematics I know. At Turing, Innodata, and previously Outlier, I design the adversarial problems that expose exactly where and why AI models fail at reasoning, then build the rubric-based grading systems that turn those failures into measurable improvements.


Lately that's meant building my own tools too — eight original research projects connecting cosmology and AI/ML directly: neural networks that emulate cosmological models 35x faster than direct integration, a physics-informed neural network built from scratch with hand-rolled automatic differentiation, and benchmark infrastructure for testing exactly how LLMs reason about physics. All public, all real data, all results reported honestly — including the unglamorous ones.

Research Focus

Matter Creation Cosmology

I explore how the universe expands through gravitationally induced matter creation — non-equilibrium thermodynamic processes within modified gravity theories, particularly f(Q) gravity. This research asks whether continuous matter creation can explain cosmic acceleration without invoking dark energy at all.

Modified Gravity Theories

My work in f(Q) gravity formalism examines alternatives to general relativity for explaining cosmic acceleration. I build generalized Chaplygin gas models and run statistical analysis against real observational datasets to test how viable these theoretical frameworks actually are.

Bulk Viscous Cosmology

Another core thread: incorporating bulk viscous dissipation into cosmological models, investigating how bulk viscosity affects cosmic dynamics alongside matter creation and exotic equations of state like the Modified Chaplygin Gas — with a close eye on thermodynamic consistency throughout.

AI in Scientific Research

I combine the physics research background above with AI/ML engineering — building benchmark infrastructure that tests LLM reasoning on physics and mathematics problems, designing automated graders that diagnose specific failure modes rather than a binary pass/fail, and applying the same statistical rigor from my cosmology work to evaluating machine intelligence. This is the thread connecting my day job to the project portfolio below.
